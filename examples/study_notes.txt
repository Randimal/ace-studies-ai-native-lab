Water moves through an ongoing cycle between the Earth's surface and atmosphere. Evaporation changes liquid water into water vapor when energy is absorbed. Transpiration releases water vapor from plant leaves. Condensation occurs when water vapor cools and forms liquid droplets. Clouds contain tiny water droplets or ice crystals suspended in the atmosphere.

Precipitation returns water to the surface as rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Infiltration is the movement of water from the surface into the soil. Groundwater occupies spaces in soil and rock below the surface. Surface runoff carries water over land into streams, rivers, and lakes. Gravity drives the movement of water downhill.

The Sun supplies much of the energy that drives evaporation. Water can remain in oceans, lakes, glaciers, and underground reservoirs for different lengths of time. These storage locations are called reservoirs. Conservation of water means protecting water quality and using available supplies responsibly. Pollution can make water unsuitable for some uses even when the total amount of water is unchanged.
